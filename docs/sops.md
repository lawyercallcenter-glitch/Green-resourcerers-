## SOP-001: Homeowner Request Intake

### Purpose
Establish a consistent, professional process for receiving and processing homeowner requests for satellite dish removal.

### Scope
Applies to all inbound service requests from residential homeowners, property managers, HOAs, and commercial property owners.

### Procedure

**1.1 Request Receipt**
- Receive request via phone, website form, email, or in-person referral
- Log the request in the CRM system with the following information:
  - Homeowner name and contact information
  - Property address
  - Number of dishes to be removed
  - Dish location(s) (roof, wall, balcony, pole-mounted)
  - Approximate dish size(s)
  - Any known access issues (steep roof, multi-story, gated property)
  - How the homeowner heard about the service
  - Preferred scheduling window

**1.2 Initial Screening**
- Verify the property is within the current service area
- Confirm the requester is the property owner or authorized representative
- Identify any obvious disqualifying conditions (active service dish, landlord restrictions)
- Flag requests that may require a pre-visit site assessment (complex access, commercial, multi-unit)

**1.3 Scheduling**
- Provide the homeowner with an estimated service window
- For standard residential removals: schedule directly if no site assessment is needed
- For complex or commercial jobs: schedule a site assessment first (see SOP-002)
- Send confirmation via email or text with date, time window, and preparation instructions

**1.4 Pre-Service Communication**
- Send reminder notification 24 hours before scheduled service
- Include instructions for the homeowner:
  - Ensure access to the dish location (unlock gates, clear obstructions)
  - Secure pets
  - Provide parking access for service vehicle
  - Be available (or designate a representative) to sign the liability waiver

### Records
- CRM entry for every request (including declined or out-of-area requests)
- Confirmation communication log

## SOP-002: Site Assessment

### Purpose
Evaluate the work site before removal to identify hazards, plan the removal approach, and provide an accurate quote for complex jobs.

### Scope
Required for all commercial properties, multi-unit buildings, and any residential property flagged during intake as complex.

### Procedure

**2.1 Pre-Visit Preparation**
- Review the CRM intake notes and any photos provided by the homeowner
- Load assessment kit: clipboard/tablet, camera, measuring tape, PPE (hard hat, safety glasses)
- Confirm appointment with homeowner

**2.2 On-Site Assessment**
- Introduce yourself and present company identification
- Walk the property exterior and identify:
  - Number and location of all dishes and associated equipment
  - Mounting type (roof penetrating, wall-mounted, chimney-mounted, pole/ground-mounted)
  - Dish size and estimated weight
  - Roof type, pitch, and condition
  - Building height and access points
  - Ladder placement options and fall hazard zones
  - Overhead electrical lines or other utilities
  - Evidence of structural damage around mounting points
  - Potential asbestos or lead paint concerns (older buildings)
- Take photographs of each dish, mounting point, and any hazards
- Note any required repairs (hole patching, sealant, siding repair)

**2.3 Assessment Documentation**
- Complete the Site Assessment Form with findings, photos, and recommended approach
- Classify the job:
  - **Standard:** Single dish, single-story, standard roof pitch, clear access
  - **Moderate:** Multiple dishes, two-story, moderate pitch, minor access challenges
  - **Complex:** Commercial, three+ stories, steep pitch, structural concerns, special equipment needed
- Generate a quote based on classification and scope

**2.4 Homeowner Communication**
- Review findings with the homeowner on-site or via follow-up call
- Provide written quote with scope of work, timeline, and price
- Obtain signed approval and liability waiver before scheduling removal

### Records
- Completed Site Assessment Form with photos
- Signed quote and waiver (stored in CRM)

## SOP-003: Satellite Dish Removal

### Purpose
Define the safe, standardized process for removing satellite dish assemblies from residential and commercial properties.

### Scope
Applies to all field removal operations performed by trained technicians.

### Required Equipment
- Extension ladder (appropriate height for the structure)
- Fall protection harness and roof anchor (for work above 6 feet)
- Cordless drill/driver with appropriate bit set
- Adjustable wrench set
- Wire cutters / cable snips
- Pry bar (small)
- Sealant / roofing caulk (for sealing bolt holes)
- Roofing patch material (matching, if possible)
- Tarps and drop cloths
- Collection bins (labeled: metals, electronics, plastics, trash)
- PPE: hard hat, safety glasses, work gloves, non-slip boots

### Procedure

**3.1 Pre-Work Setup**
- Arrive on-site and confirm the homeowner or representative is present
- Obtain signed liability waiver if not already on file
- Conduct a brief safety assessment:
  - Check weather conditions (do not work in rain, high winds, or lightning)
  - Inspect ladder placement area for level ground, overhead hazards
  - Identify fall zones and set up perimeter cones or caution tape if needed
- Lay tarps below the work area to catch debris
- Brief all crew members on the removal plan

**3.2 Cable Disconnection**
- Trace the coaxial cable from the dish to the point of entry into the building
- Disconnect the cable at the grounding block or exterior junction
- Coil and remove exterior cable runs
- Cap or seal any cable entry points into the building

**3.3 Dish and Mount Removal**
- Secure the ladder and ascend to the dish location (with fall protection as required)
- Disconnect the LNB (low-noise block downconverter) arm and set aside
- Remove the dish reflector from the mounting bracket
- Lower the dish to the ground using a rope or hand-off to a ground crew member — **never throw or drop components**
- Remove the mounting bracket and all associated hardware (bolts, lag screws, clamps)
- Inspect the mounting surface for damage

**3.4 Surface Restoration**
- Fill all bolt holes and penetrations with appropriate sealant or roofing cement
- Apply roofing patch material over sealed penetrations on roof surfaces
- For wall mounts: fill holes with exterior-grade filler and note if painting is needed
- Photograph the restored surface for documentation

**3.5 Site Cleanup**
- Remove all debris, hardware, cable, and components from the property
- Sweep or clean the work area
- Remove tarps, cones, and equipment
- Conduct a final walk-around with the homeowner to confirm satisfaction

**3.6 Component Handling**
- Sort removed components into labeled bins:
  - **Metals:** Aluminum dish, steel mounts, copper wiring
  - **Electronics:** LNB, circuit boards, connectors
  - **Plastics:** Dish housing, cable sheathing
  - **Trash:** Non-recyclable residue
- Transport sorted materials to the processing facility (see SOP-004)

### Safety Requirements
- Two-person minimum for all roof work
- Fall protection required for any work above 6 feet
- No work during inclement weather (rain, wind > 25 mph, lightning within 10 miles)
- All technicians must have completed safety training and hold current OSHA 10 certification

### Records
- Completed Job Ticket with arrival/departure times, work performed, and materials removed
- Before and after photographs
- Signed homeowner satisfaction confirmation
- Material inventory loaded to processing log

## SOP-004: Material Sorting and Processing

### Purpose
Define the process for sorting, categorizing, and preparing recovered materials for recycling, refining, or disposal.

### Scope
Applies to all materials returned to the processing facility from field operations.

### Procedure

**4.1 Receiving**
- Unload materials from the service vehicle into the receiving area
- Verify material inventory against the Job Ticket from the removal
- Log the date, source property, and total weight received

**4.2 Primary Sorting**
- Separate materials into the following categories:

| Category | Materials | Destination |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Ferrous metals | Steel mounts, brackets, hardware | Scrap metal recycler |
| Non-ferrous metals | Aluminum dishes, copper wire | Scrap metal recycler |
| Precious metal bearing | LNBs, circuit boards, connectors, gold-plated contacts | Precious metal refiner (SOP-005) |
| Plastics | Dish housings, cable sheathing, covers | Plastics recycler |
| Cables | Coaxial cable, wiring harnesses | Cable recycler / copper recovery |
| Hazardous | Batteries, capacitors (if present) | Hazardous waste disposal (SOP-006) |
| Non-recyclable | Mixed residue, damaged beyond recovery | Landfill disposal (SOP-006) |

**4.3 Secondary Processing**
- Disassemble LNBs and separate circuit boards from housings
- Strip cable sheathing to expose copper (if processing in-house)
- Remove and collect gold-plated connectors and contacts
- Weigh and record each material category

**4.4 Storage**
- Store sorted materials in labeled, covered bins or containers
- Keep precious-metal-bearing materials in a secure, locked area
- Maintain a running inventory of stored materials by category and weight
- Schedule batch pickups or drop-offs with recycling and refining partners when volume thresholds are met

### Records
- Material Receiving Log (date, source, weight, category breakdown)
- Running inventory by material category
- Batch shipment records

## SOP-005: Precious Metal Recovery

### Purpose
Define the process for identifying, accumulating, and sending precious-metal-bearing materials to a certified refiner for recovery.

### Scope
Applies to all electronic components recovered from satellite dish assemblies that contain precious metals (gold, silver, palladium, platinum).

### Procedure

**5.1 Identification**
- Identify precious-metal-bearing components during sorting (SOP-004):
  - Circuit boards (gold traces, gold-plated edge connectors)
  - LNB assemblies (gold and silver in RF components)
  - Coaxial connectors (gold plating)
  - Multilayer ceramic capacitors (palladium)
  - Solder (silver content)

**5.2 Preparation**
- Remove and separate precious-metal-bearing components from non-bearing materials
- Remove large non-metallic components (plastic housings, heatsinks without plating) to increase concentration
- Do **not** attempt chemical extraction or smelting in-house without proper licensing, equipment, and training
- Weigh and photograph each batch

**5.3 Testing (Optional)**
- Use XRF (X-ray fluorescence) analyzer to estimate precious metal content (if available)
- Alternatively, use acid test kits for basic gold/silver verification
- Record test results for comparison with refiner assay reports

**5.4 Batch Accumulation**
- Accumulate precious-metal-bearing materials until a shipment threshold is reached (typically 10–50 lbs of circuit boards)
- Store securely in a locked area with access limited to authorized personnel

**5.5 Refiner Shipment**
- Select a reputable, certified precious metal refiner (verify certifications: R2, e-Stewards, or equivalent)
- Package materials securely for shipping per refiner instructions
- Insure the shipment for estimated value
- Ship via tracked, insured carrier
- Retain tracking number and shipping receipt

**5.6 Settlement**
- Receive assay report from the refiner detailing precious metal content and weight
- Compare assay results with internal estimates
- Review and approve settlement statement
- Receive payment per agreed terms (check, wire, or credit)
- Log the transaction with full details (batch ID, weight sent, assay results, payment received)

### Records
- Precious metal batch log (batch ID, components, weight, date accumulated)
- Shipment records (carrier, tracking, insurance)
- Refiner assay reports
- Settlement statements and payment records

## SOP-006: Environmental Disposal

### Purpose
Ensure all non-recyclable and hazardous materials are disposed of in compliance with federal, state, and local environmental regulations.

### Scope
Applies to all waste materials generated during removal and processing operations that cannot be recycled or recovered.

### Procedure

**6.1 Waste Classification**
- Classify all waste materials according to EPA and state guidelines:

| Classification | Examples | Handling |
|---------------|----------|---------|
| **Non-hazardous solid waste** | Mixed residue, broken plastics, non-recyclable composites | Licensed landfill |
| **Universal waste** | Batteries, mercury-containing components (if any) | Universal waste handler |
| **Hazardous waste** | Capacitors containing PCBs (rare in satellite dishes, but possible in older equipment) | Licensed hazardous waste transporter and facility |

**6.2 Non-Hazardous Disposal**
- Accumulate non-recyclable solid waste in designated containers
- Transport to a licensed municipal landfill or transfer station
- Obtain and retain disposal receipts with weight documentation
- Minimize landfill volume by maximizing recycling and recovery in SOP-004

**6.3 Universal Waste Handling**
- Store universal waste (batteries, etc.) in labeled, closed containers
- Do not exceed accumulation time limits (typically one year per EPA guidelines)
- Transport to a licensed universal waste handler or recycler
- Maintain documentation per state universal waste regulations

**6.4 Hazardous Waste Handling**
- If hazardous waste is identified (e.g., PCB-containing capacitors):
  - Isolate the material immediately in a labeled, sealed container
  - Do **not** mix with other waste streams
  - Contact a licensed hazardous waste transporter for pickup
  - Complete a hazardous waste manifest (EPA Form 8700-22)
  - Retain manifest copies for a minimum of three years
- If uncertain about classification, treat the material as hazardous until tested

**6.5 Regulatory Compliance**
- Maintain current knowledge of applicable regulations:
  - EPA RCRA (Resource Conservation and Recovery Act)
  - State environmental agency rules for e-waste and recycling
  - Local ordinances for waste transport and disposal
- Obtain and maintain all required permits (EPA ID, state recycling permit, waste transporter permit)
- Train all staff on waste identification and handling procedures

### Records
- Waste disposal receipts (landfill, recycler, hazardous waste facility)
- Hazardous waste manifests (if applicable)
- Waste volume tracking log (weight and classification)
- Permit and license copies (current)

## SOP-007: Quality Control and Reporting

### Purpose
Ensure consistent service quality, accurate data collection, and timely reporting to support operations, grant compliance, and continuous improvement.

### Scope
Applies to all operational activities, from intake through disposal and reporting.

### Procedure

**7.1 Job-Level Quality Control**
- Every completed job must include:
  - Before and after photographs of each removal site
  - Signed homeowner satisfaction confirmation
  - Completed Job Ticket with all fields filled
  - Material inventory matching the removal
- Supervisors review a random sample of 20% of completed jobs weekly for:
  - Photo quality and completeness
  - Surface restoration quality
  - Accurate material documentation
  - Customer satisfaction follow-up (phone or email within 48 hours)

**7.2 Material Tracking Quality Control**
- Cross-reference Job Ticket material inventories with processing facility Receiving Logs
- Investigate discrepancies exceeding 10% by weight
- Audit precious metal batch logs against refiner assay reports quarterly

**7.3 Safety Quality Control**
- Conduct monthly safety inspections of:
  - Vehicles and equipment
  - PPE condition and availability
  - Processing facility organization and hazard controls
- Review all incident reports within 24 hours
- Conduct root cause analysis for any safety incident
- Maintain zero-incident goal; report all near-misses

**7.4 Operational Reporting**

| Report | Frequency | Contents | Audience |
|--------|-----------|----------|----------|
| Daily Activity Log | Daily | Jobs completed, materials collected, issues encountered | Operations manager |
| Weekly Operations Summary | Weekly | Volume, revenue, material recovery, safety, customer feedback | Management team |
| Monthly Performance Report | Monthly | KPIs vs. targets, financials, material recovery yields, customer satisfaction | Management, stakeholders |
| Quarterly Impact Report | Quarterly | Environmental metrics, community impact, grant deliverables | Grant agencies, partners, public |
| Annual Business Review | Annually | Comprehensive financial, operational, and impact review | Owners, investors, grant agencies |

**7.5 Continuous Improvement**
- Review customer feedback weekly; identify trends and recurring issues
- Hold monthly team meetings to discuss operational improvements
- Update SOPs at least annually or whenever a process change is implemented
- Document all SOP revisions with version number, date, and summary of changes

### Records
- Quality control audit logs
- Incident and near-miss reports
- All reports listed in section 7.4
- SOP revision history
